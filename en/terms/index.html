Hence the practical advice the app also shows on your first upload: only upload your own photos or images you are allowed to share. For everything else there is the route via a link — an entry with an address fetches the title and preview image from the source instead of making a copy.

Reporting content

Shared lists are accessible by invitation only; nothing in Decito is public. Anyone who can see a list can report any entry in it directly in the app — and any member can also delete an entry themselves.

If you are affected yourself and are not a member — for example, as the creator of an image —, an email to hello@splash.green is enough. For me to be able to act, the report must contain (Art. 16(2) DSA):

  • a statement of reasons why the content is unlawful;
  • information that allows it to be located — the list, the entry, the time or the image itself; there is no public address, because nothing is publicly posted;
  • your name and your email address;
  • a confirmation that the information is accurate and complete to the best of your knowledge.

I review every report and remove or block access to whatever must be removed. The affected person receives a statement of reasons and information about their available means of redress (Art. 17 DSA). Until a report is made, I know nothing about a piece of content — that is the role that Section 10 DDG and Art. 6 DSA assign to a hosting service, and I do not monitor anything preemptively.

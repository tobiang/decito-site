Privacy Policy

This is a convenience translation. Only the German version is legally binding: Datenschutzerklärung.

Last updated: November 21, 2026

Controller

Decito is developed and operated by a single individual. The controller within the meaning of the GDPR is Tobias Langen, Germany.
Email: hello@splash.green
There is no data protection officer; the requirements of Art. 37 GDPR are not met.

In short

Decito contains no analytics, tracking, advertising or crash-reporting libraries. There are no advertising IDs, no profiling across providers and no resale of data. What is processed is what you create and do in the app — such as what you completed or planned — and what operating it technically requires. An error report leaves your device only if you send it yourself — see Feedback and error reports.

This policy applies to the app and to this website. Where something applies to only one of the two, this is stated there.

Not end-to-end encrypted: I can technically get at your data, but I only look when you ask me to, when something is reported, when a fault cannot be fixed any other way or when a law requires it.

This website

The pages are served by GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA). When you access them, your browser transmits technically necessary data, which GitHub stores in server logs — in particular your IP address, date and time, the requested address and browser and system identifiers. I have no access to this and do not evaluate any of it. GitHub decides itself what it uses these logs for (for example the security of its service), how long it keeps them and whether they go to the USA; I am not involved. GitHub’s privacy statement has the details.

The website sets no cookies, embeds no third-party fonts, maps or videos, and loads nothing from any other server.

Legal basis for serving the pages through GitHub: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR — the legitimate interest in being able to serve them at all and keep them stable.

Account and sign-in

You can use Decito without an email address: in that case, an account is created that consists of nothing more than a random identifier and a code that, in the app, only you see (see Your rights). If you sign in via magic link, your email address is stored as well. You also choose a display name; the app asks for it once on first launch, because otherwise nobody in a shared list would know who a contribution came from. What you enter there is up to you — a first name is enough, as is a nickname; it does not have to be your name. The app also transmits your device’s time zone — its name, such as “Europe/Berlin”, and its offset from UTC —, so that “today” is your calendar day and not the server’s, across a daylight-saving change too.

Optionally, you can give your profile a color and a symbol; the others see both next to your name in your shared lists. Equally optionally, you choose a federal state: it determines which public holidays and school holidays apply under “Allowed days” in your lists, and with it the app tells you on Home, in “Plans” and in the day notifications what kind of day it is. For these notifications, it stores the names of the upcoming days and your federal state on your device. If a public holiday applies there only in part of the state, you decide yourself whether it counts for you — only that choice is then stored, never your place of residence. The members of your lists see your federal state, because it determines when entries in them come back.

The app asks for nothing more: apart from the display name, no information is required, and without an email address Decito works fully, except for signing in on a second device.

There is no sign-in via third parties — Google, Apple, social networks. So no account with any such provider is touched, and none of them learns that you use Decito.

An account without an email address is reachable only from the device on which it was created — on iOS, also from an iPhone restored from that device’s encrypted backup. On any other device, nobody can prove they are entitled to it, and I cannot give you access back. If the device is lost without such a backup, nobody can get in any more. That is why such an account that has not been used for two years is deleted together with its contents. Every launch of the app resets this period, as soon as the app reaches the server in doing so — opening it is enough. So does ticking off or postponing an entry from the home screen widget or from a notification. For this purpose, I store when the account was last used this way (to the nearest half day). The app reminds you in good time and several times beforehand, and consistently offers to add and confirm an email address; after that, the period no longer applies, and the time is no longer updated — the last one stored remains.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (provision of the features you request).

If you do not agree to changed terms of use, I keep your account and your content, so that you can agree later and carry on, and the members of your shared lists keep their content. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR.

As with any online service, Supabase (see Recipients) learns your device’s IP address, the program’s identifier and the time with every connection the app makes to the server, and records them in its access logs. For each sign-in session, Supabase also stores the device’s IP address and program identifier for as long as the session exists. I do not evaluate any of this; it serves operation and the detection of abuse. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR — the legitimate interest in running the service securely and stably.

Content you create

Everything you record in a list (in the app: a collection) is stored — currently, for example, entries, notes, tags, dates, ratings, polls and uploaded images. This content is visible to the members of the respective list — that is the purpose of the app. Anyone who shares a list with you sees the associated contributions and your display name. In a running poll, members see who has finished and whom the poll is still waiting for. Nobody sees how the others rated while the poll runs; once it ends, every member of the list sees who rated how — including those who did not vote themselves. If you choose “Count me out” or switch off “Count me in polls” in a list, that is stored; only you can see it, the others merely see that the poll is not waiting for you.

What you write in a free text field or upload as an image is your decision. If you store information about other people there, you are processing it — and you answer for the rights to an uploaded image; the Terms of Use say what that means.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

How you use the app

I store when an entry was completed — if you undo it right away, the record disappears again — and how you responded to suggestions. Like the rest of your content, this data is held at Supabase (see Recipients). The app computes the recommendations and statistics from it on your device, and does so within your own lists: they are not combined with other services, not linked into a profile across lists and do not serve any advertising. In a shared list, your yes and no responses to an entry feed into the shared tally of opinion that the other members see — that is what they are for.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

Thanks

When you thank another member for what they did in a shared list on a given day, I store who thanked whom, for which day in which list, which entry it names, and the hour you gave it. The thank-you is delivered to the other person live. Only the two of you can see it, each of you only while you are yourself a member of the list; other members learn nothing about it.

So that work which would otherwise go unseen can be thanked too, the app shows the other members of a shared list what someone did there on a day in the past week — completed, planned, wrote down or filled in — as one line with a “Thanks”, never as a count or a ranking. For this, I also store who planned an entry and who filled an entry in — a photo, a link or a few words —, each with the hour, but not what was changed. So that covering for someone counts too: if you take over an entry that was assigned to another member, I store whom it was assigned to before and when you took it over; and for each completed entry, whose task it was at the time. Members of the list can see this.

Only on your device, the app keeps for this which thank-yous you have already seen, which thanks lines you hid and when you last opened the app on this device; this information does not leave your device.

Legal basis: for a thank-you, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR; for the line and for who planned, filled in or took over an entry and whose task it was, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR — the members’ legitimate interest in seeing and acknowledging work that would otherwise go unnoticed.

No tracking across providers

Decito does not track you across other apps or websites. It reads no advertising identifier — neither the Advertising ID on Android nor the IDFA on iOS —, embeds no third-party measurement tools and passes nothing to advertising networks or data brokers. That is also why the app does not ask for permission to track on iOS (App Tracking Transparency): there is nothing it would need to ask for.

On your device

The app keeps a local copy of your data so that it can show something immediately after launch, along with settings such as sorting and display, and the sign-in session. Personal intentions such as food focus and mindful shopping also stay on the device and do not reach the server. For tag suggestions, a language model is downloaded once from the file storage at Supabase (see Recipients) and then run exclusively on the device. The suggestion feature can be turned off in the settings.

If you want to take text from a photo, the app uses your device’s text recognition — on Android, that of Google Play services (ML Kit), whose recognition model the operating system downloads from Google as needed; on iOS, the one Apple ships with the system, for which nothing is downloaded. The recognition itself runs exclusively on the device, the photo does not leave it for this, and none of this happens without your action: the feature becomes active only when you invoke it.

To follow an invitation, you can scan the QR code on the inviting person’s device with the camera. The camera is opened only for this process and closed again afterwards. Recognizing the code is handled by your device’s built-in capabilities — on Android, Google Play services (ML Kit), which download the recognition model from Google as needed; on iOS, the system functions supplied by Apple, for which nothing is downloaded. In both cases, the analysis runs exclusively on the device, no image is stored and none is transmitted, and only the invitation ID contained in the code is used.

None of this serves analysis or re-identification; all of it is necessary for the feature you invoked, which is why it falls under the exception in Section 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG and does not require consent. When you uninstall the app, this local data is removed from the device — except for a device backup, if you have one.

Your operating system may include this local copy in your device’s backup — on Android in your Google account, on iOS in your iCloud. This backup is held in your own account with the respective provider and on its terms; I have no access to it. Whether it takes place is up to you in the system settings.

Entries with a link

If you share a link from another app to Decito, type one in, or have an entry reloaded from its source, Decito accesses that address directly from your device to determine the title and preview image; for IMDb links, it accesses IMDb’s suggestion interface instead of the page. In doing so, the website accessed learns your IP address and a generic browser identifier (user agent), just as with any access in a browser. No intermediate server is used, and I receive no data through this.

It does not stop at this one access: the preview image and site icon are stored as an address, not as a copy. Both are therefore fetched from the original website — on the device of every member who opens the list, not just on yours; after that, each device shows them from its local cache until that cache is cleared. With each retrieval, the website learns the IP address of the respective device. Without an entry with a link, none of this takes place.

Legal basis: for the retrieval on your device, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR; for the retrieval on the other members’ devices, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR — the interest in showing an image at its source instead of making a copy to which I hold no rights.

The map

A collection for going out or for activities can show its entries on a map. The app loads the map image and its fonts from the file storage at Supabase (see Recipients); in doing so, Supabase learns your device’s IP address and which part of the map it loads, as with any other access. No map provider such as Google is involved, and your location is requested only when you tap “My location” on the map: it is shown there, stays on your device and is neither stored nor sent.

If an entry only has an address, you can bring it onto the map with “Find with Apple Maps” or “Find with Google”. Only then does the app send the addresses of those entries to your operating system’s location service — on an iPhone to Apple, on Android to the service your device provides for it, usually Google — together with your device’s IP address. The app writes the coordinates it finds as a line into the entry’s text, where every member of the collection sees them. Without that tap, no address is looked up.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

Notifications

Reminders are generated by the device itself from data it already has. There is no push service, no device identifier for notifications and therefore no recipient either: nothing leaves the device, and you can revoke the permission at any time in the operating system. A timer from a recipe step is handed, on your tap, with its label to your device’s clock, which runs it from then on — this too stays on the device.

No automated decisions

No automated decision within the meaning of Art. 22 GDPR takes place. The app’s recommendations order your own entries according to your own input — they do not evaluate any person, have no legal effect, and you can disregard any suggestion.

Reports and moderation

Decito stores content on behalf of the people who use it and is therefore a hosting service within the meaning of the Digital Services Act (DSA). You can report content using the reporting feature in the app (Art. 16 DSA); anyone who is not a member of the list — for example, the creator of an image — can reach me by email, see Reporting content. If something is removed or blocked as a result, the affected person receives a statement of reasons and information about their available means of redress (Art. 17 DSA). Shared lists are accessible only by invitation and thus do not disseminate anything to the public; the additional obligations for online platforms therefore do not apply.

I store the report, the title and image address of the reported entry and the IDs of the reporting and the uploading person; for a report by email, additionally the name and email address of the reporting person, which Art. 16(2) DSA requires for an actionable report — except for reports about the sexual abuse of children, which can be made without a name. In addition, a moderation log is kept for every measure.

The report and the log are retained even after an account is deleted and contain the ID and the display name of the uploading person as it was at the time of the report; the ID of the reporting person is removed when that person’s account is deleted. Both are automatically deleted after three years — the report three years after it was received, the log three years after the measure. Without this exception, a measure could be erased without a trace by deleting one’s own account — and a statement of reasons that is challenged later could no longer be verified.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(c) and (f) GDPR; retention despite a request for erasure is based on Art. 17(3)(e) GDPR.

Feedback and error reports

In the app, you can write feedback to me and manually send an error that occurred as a report. None of this happens automatically: the app contains no crash-reporting library, and an error leaves your device only if you tap Send yourself.

With an entry, the following is stored: your text; for an error report, the error message and the technical trace (stack trace) together with the place in the app last opened; in addition, the app version, processor architecture, time zone, operating system version, language setting, device model and the status of the notification permission — as well as your account ID.

You can see your entries again in the app, together with any response from me (under review, resolved or declined, with at most one note). You can mark any entry as resolved and delete it at any time; when your account is deleted, all your entries disappear. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR — legitimate interest in a working product, with the transmission always initiated by you.

When you write to me

If you send me an email — about an access request, a deletion or a report, say —, I process it together with your sender address to deal with your request and to be able to show later that I did. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR insofar as it concerns your rights under the GDPR or a report under the DSA, otherwise Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR — the legitimate interest in being able to reply to you. I delete the correspondence at the end of the third year after the year in which the matter was closed — until then, claims arising from it could still be made (§§ 195, 199 BGB).

Retention period

  • Account with an email address and its content: until you delete the account.
  • Account without an email address and its content: until you delete it, at the latest two years after last use.
  • Content in shared lists: what you added there stays in the list after your account is deleted, without your name.
  • Reports about content: three years from receipt.
  • Feedback and error reports: until you delete the entry, at the latest when your account is deleted.
  • Moderation log: three years from the measure.
  • A thank-you: 30 days, and immediately with your account.
  • Who filled an entry in: eight days, and immediately with your account.
  • Who planned an entry: as long as the entry has a day, even after it is done; when your account is deleted, the entry loses its link to you.
  • Whom a taken-over entry was assigned to before: until it is assigned anew; whose task it was when completed: eight days. When your account is deleted, both lose their link to you.
  • Local data on the device: until uninstallation or until you clear the app’s storage; the names of the upcoming days also as soon as you sign out.
  • Access logs at Supabase: according to Supabase’s retention periods; a sign-in session including its IP address, for as long as it exists, at most until your account is deleted.
  • Emails to me: until the end of the third year after the year in which the matter was closed.
  • Server logs of this website: according to GitHub’s retention periods, over which I have no influence.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account yourself in the app under “Account” (the person icon at the top of the home screen); image files from lists deleted along with it are gone by the following day at the latest. The account, profile and your content are removed — with the exception of the records described under Reports and moderation. What you added to shared lists — including your own lists that pass to a member — stays in those lists and is shown there without your name, because the other members keep planning with it (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The same applies to completion, planning and poll data already recorded there. If a photo or text shows you yourself and should go, delete it beforehand or write to me, and I will delete it. Thank-yous you gave or received, the record of which entries you filled in, and your verdicts on entries (stars and remark) are deleted, including in other people’s lists.

Lists that belong to you and still have someone else in them pass to the member who has belonged to them the longest — the others’ contributions depend on them. Only lists in which there is no one else are deleted along with it. The deletion takes you out of an ongoing poll; the others keep voting, and if only your vote was missing, it ends.

The same holds when the two-year period of an account without an email address expires and there is no one who could be asked: a shared list passes to the member who has belonged to it the longest — the others did not agree to its disappearance. That member sees a notice the next time they open the app that the list has passed to them. Only lists in which there is no one else are deleted along with it.

It also works without the app: Delete account without the app describes the route by email.

Security

Your data is held at Supabase in Frankfurt, encrypted in transit and at rest, but not end-to-end: not in a way that only the list members’ devices can read it. Technically, as the operator, I can get at everything on the server; Supabase processes it only on my instructions. I look at content only if you ask me to, if something is reported — and then only what I need to locate and assess the report —, if a fault or an attack cannot be fixed any other way, or if a law obliges me to. I do not go looking through your data, sell nothing and train no models on it.

Which row belongs to which account is decided by the database itself through access rules that apply to every query — not by the app. No account that is not a member of a list receives its content — not even with a hand-crafted request to the database.

Age

Anyone aged 13 or over may use Decito; anyone not yet 18 needs the consent of their parents or other legal representatives. I do not check anyone’s age. If I learn that someone is younger than 13 or that their parents do not consent, I delete the account — parents can write to me informally at hello@splash.green. Nothing in Decito is public: a list is seen only by those invited to it.

Your rights

You have the right of access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and objection (Art. 21 GDPR). For access and data portability, an email to hello@splash.green is enough; for data portability, you receive your data in a commonly used, machine-readable format (Art. 20 GDPR).

Right to object (Art. 21 GDPR): Insofar as data is processed on the basis of Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, you may object at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation. This covers:

  • serving this website through GitHub;
  • the access logs and sign-in sessions at Supabase;
  • retrieving preview images on the other members’ devices;
  • the “Thanks” line and who planned, filled in or took over an entry and whose task it was;
  • keeping your account if you do not agree to changed terms of use, and your contributions in shared lists after a deletion;
  • keeping reports and the moderation log;
  • feedback and error reports;
  • emails to me.

A short informal email to hello@splash.green is enough.

If you use Decito without an email address, I cannot identify you without additional information; in that case, Art. 15–20 GDPR apply only insofar as you provide the missing information (Art. 11(2) GDPR). That is this account’s code, which the app shows under “Account” — give it to me, and your request can be answered. The earlier “ID” that older versions of the app show is no longer enough, because it also reaches the members of your shared lists.

You can also lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority, in particular in the federal state of your habitual residence.
